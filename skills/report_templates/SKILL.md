# Research Atlas Report Templates

Use the template that matches the task. Do not fill empty sections with generic prose. If evidence is insufficient, state that the section is not assessed or omit optional sections.

## Template A — SC01 three-source weekly brief

1. **Period and executive synthesis:** 3–5 sentences on the most consequential research developments and why they matter.
2. **L0 coverage and evidence depth:** unique records, A/B/C counts, source tiers, time window, pagination/limits, publication types, abstract/full-text availability and failures. Explain denominators.
3. **A — Planning applications:** one or more L1 research signals, or explicitly no strong signal.
4. **B — Urban-science framing:** one or more L1 signals about concepts, constructs, measurement, scale, mechanisms or competing explanations.
5. **C — Computational methods:** one or more L1 signals about method capability, theory, benchmarks or evaluation; explain the urban problem each could address without assuming transfer.
6. **L2 selective deep reads:** only papers that materially change understanding; may be empty.
7. **L3 optional research actions:** monitor, deep-read, compare, reproduce, adapt or propose a topic; may be empty.
8. **Evidence index and audit appendix:** paper IDs, DOI/URLs, venue identity, evidence level, source group, claim links, unresolved identities and retrieval limitations.

Main text is organized by research themes, not paper-by-paper summaries. Do not force equal topic counts across groups.

## Template B — L1 cross-paper research signal

### Signal title
A concise statement of the research development or unresolved issue.

### Why it matters
Explain the scientific or planning consequence, not merely that the topic is popular.

### What the papers jointly show
Compare at least two distinct papers. Explain the shared direction and the important differences in question, object, scale, data, method or outcome.

### What remains unresolved
State the gap and its status: not assessed, author claim only, or compared with prior art. Name the closest works and the evidence that limits the claim.

### Evidence and disagreement
For each key claim, cite paper IDs. Include counterexamples, conflicting results and alternative interpretations.

### Conditional relevance
Explain what the signal could mean for the team's research lines, methods or registered data metadata. State the required conditions and unknowns.

### Recommended action
Choose a concrete action and explain why now. If no action is justified, say so.

### Representative papers
List each paper's role: direct evidence, method development, validation, counterexample, nearest neighbor or background. Include DOI/URL and evidence level.

## Template C — L2 single-paper deep review

- **Why:** motivation and importance of the research problem.
- **Gap:** what is missing relative to the actual prior baseline.
- **How:** data, unit of analysis, method mechanism, inputs and outputs.
- **What's different:** the substantive methodological or conceptual change.
- **Does it work:** baselines, metrics, results, validation and limitations visible in the evidence.
- **Team fit:** direct use, conditional adaptation or non-transferable; state why.
- **Next:** what to inspect, compare or test next.
- **Evidence boundary:** metadata, abstract or full text; identify unknowns.

## Template D — SC03 / UC01 research opportunity report

1. **Executive verdict:** GO / HOLD / REJECT; one main opportunity at most, up to two alternatives, or no recommendation.
2. **Task scope and evidence boundary:** seed paper, intended research line, constraints, source snapshot and stages actually completed.
3. **Seed-paper reconstruction:** question, object, construct, data, unit, method, findings, author claims and limitations.
4. **Research diagnosis:** explicit source-paper gaps, evidence-supported implications and agent hypotheses kept separate.
5. **Literature evidence map:** direct, indirect, counterexample and unlinked papers; identity and evidence status.
6. **Candidate-route comparison:** problem importance, mechanism, contribution type, method, data, nearest neighbors and risks.
7. **Main research opportunity:** research question, hypothesis, continuity with seed paper, substantive contribution and why it could sustain a paper.
8. **Method-transfer chain:** source capability, mechanism bridge, inputs/outputs, assumptions, adaptation, baselines and failure boundaries.
9. **Minimum viable study design:** data types and possible sources, unit/scale, comparison groups, baseline, metrics, validation and stop conditions.
10. **Nearest-neighbor novelty audit:** 3–5 closest works; what each already solves and the precise remaining increment.
11. **Feasibility and risks:** data access, sample, labels, permissions, resources, alternative explanations and unresolved prerequisites.
12. **Next actions:** ordered, decision-relevant actions; distinguish what the agent has done from what the researcher must do.
13. **References and evidence index:** stable titles, DOI/URLs, source group, publication type and evidence level.

## Required quality audit for every final report

Score 0–5 with evidence and reasons:
- continuity with the seed research: 25%;
- substantive research increment: 20%;
- literature and novelty evidence: 20%;
- method-transfer validity: 15%;
- conceptual feasibility: 10%;
- scope discipline and uncertainty: 10%.

Provisional pass: at least 75/100 and at least 3/5 for continuity, literature/novelty evidence and scope discipline. Mark unscorable items as unscorable; never invent scores.

Critical failures: fabricated paper/DOI/source; wrong paper identity or duplicate versions counted as separate works; abstract claims presented as full-text facts; one paper called a trend; keyword-only method transfer; novelty declared without nearest-neighbor review; proposed design presented as a completed experiment; unverified team data access or capability presented as fact.
